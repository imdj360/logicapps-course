<Step id="problem" name="Two rules from security" tag="PROBLEM">
The security team sets two rules for the order logic app:

1. **Nothing public.** No public endpoint on the app or its storage account.
2. **Reach the ERP.** The ERP's SQL Server sits in the company datacenter.

Right now the app is public, its storage is public, and it can't see the datacenter at all.
</Step>

<Step id="map" name="The whole map">
Here's where we're heading: the same logic app, with everything private.

- Callers come in through a **private endpoint** in your virtual network.
- The app's calls go out through an **integration subnet** in the same network.
- Its **storage account** is reached through four private endpoints.
- **Managed connectors** still run in Azure, outside your network.
- The **datacenter** is reached over the network, or through the **on-premises data gateway**.

The logic app itself **doesn't run inside your network** on a Workflow Service Plan: it runs on Azure's shared App Service infrastructure, and the private endpoint and VNet integration connect it in both directions. On an **App Service Environment v3** it does run inside your network.

The next steps take one path at a time.
</Step>

<Step id="inbound" name="Path 1: calls in">
A **private endpoint** gives the logic app a private address inside your virtual network. Callers on the network use it; from outside, calls get **403 Forbidden**.

Lesson: *Lock down inbound: private endpoints*.
</Step>

<Step id="outbound" name="Path 2: calls out">
**VNet integration** routes the app's outbound calls through a subnet in your network, so it can reach private addresses such as an internal API, including in networks **peered** to it.

Inbound and outbound are **independent** features: you can use either one or both.

Lesson: *Reach private systems: VNet integration*.
</Step>

<Step id="storage" name="Path 3: its own storage">
A Standard logic app **requires** a storage account for its artifacts. To make it private, the storage account gets **separate private endpoints for Table, Queue, Blob and File**, and the app reaches them through VNet integration.

Lesson: *The storage account on a private network*.
</Step>

<Step id="managed" name="Path 4: managed connectors">
**Managed** connectors run in Azure, outside your network. If a firewall, NSG or route table controls the app's outbound traffic, allow outbound connections to the **managed connector IP addresses** for your region.

And with a private inbound endpoint, **managed webhook triggers** can't call back in.

Lessons: *VNet integration* and *private endpoints*.
</Step>

<Step id="onprem" name="Path 5: the datacenter">
Two routes:

- **Built-in** service provider connectors, such as SQL Server, reach on-premises data sources **directly**, without the gateway, as long as the network reaches them. A VPN or ExpressRoute link from your virtual network to the datacenter is the usual path (ours).
- If there's **no built-in** connector but a **managed** one exists, you need the **on-premises data gateway**.

Lesson: *Reaching on-premises systems*.
</Step>

<Step id="order" name="Where to start" tag="TIP">
A practical order (ours): VNet integration first, because the storage and on-premises paths depend on it; then private storage; then the inbound private endpoint last, because it changes how you deploy and test.

Before go-live, check the firewall rules, subnets, fixed outbound IP and Key Vault access: the last lesson in this module, *Firewalls, fixed IPs and service tags*.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- **Inbound**: private endpoint. **Outbound**: VNet integration. They're independent.
- **Storage**: four private endpoints, reached through VNet integration.
- **Managed connectors**: outside your network; allow their IP addresses.
- **On-premises**: built-in connectors over the network, or the data gateway for managed ones.
</Step>
