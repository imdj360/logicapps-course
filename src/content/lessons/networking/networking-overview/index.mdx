<Step id="problem" name="Every project has different rules" tag="PROBLEM">
One project only connects SaaS apps. The next must reach a database on a private network. A third has security rules: nothing public, a firewall for all traffic, and files dropped in the company datacenter.

There's no single "right" network setup. Each requirement adds a piece.
</Step>

<Step id="idea" name="The idea">
**Treat networking as building blocks: start from your requirements and add only the blocks they call for.**

Each block solves one problem, brings its own trade-offs, and has a lesson in this module. You can mix them freely.
</Step>

<Step id="baseline" name="Baseline: public">
With no network setup, the logic app is public: callers reach its endpoint over the internet, and it calls services over the internet.

On a Workflow Service Plan the app runs on Azure's **shared App Service infrastructure**, outside your virtual network. Microsoft says you can't connect that network directly to yours; instead you add features for each direction. On an **App Service Environment v3** the app does run inside your network.

**Enough when:** you only connect SaaS and public APIs.
</Step>

<Step id="block-outbound" name="Block: reach private resources">
**Need:** call a private API or database, in your network, a **peered** network, or over **ExpressRoute**.

**Add:** **VNet integration** for outbound calls, plus **private DNS** for anything behind a private endpoint. To lock an Azure service such as Storage or Service Bus to your subnet without a private endpoint, a **service endpoint** is the lighter option; Microsoft recommends private endpoints for private access.

**Trade-off:** by default all outbound traffic goes through your network, so your NSGs and route tables apply to it.

Lesson: *Reach private systems: VNet integration*.
</Step>

<Step id="block-inbound" name="Block: no public inbound">
**Need:** only callers on your network may call the workflows.

**Add:** a **private endpoint**.

**Trade-off:** managed **webhook** triggers can't call in, and deployment from VS Code or the CLI must come from inside the network.

Lesson: *Lock down inbound: private endpoints*.
</Step>

<Step id="block-storage" name="Block: private storage">
**Need:** no storage account with public access.

**Add:** private endpoints for **Table, Queue, Blob and File**, reached through VNet integration (so this block needs the outbound block).

**Trade-off:** deployment needs temporary public access or an **ARM template**.

Lesson: *The storage account on a private network*.
</Step>

<Step id="block-onprem" name="Block: the datacenter">
**Need:** read or write data in the company datacenter.

**Add one of:**
- a **built-in** connector over the network: VNet integration plus **ExpressRoute** or a **site-to-site VPN** from your network to the datacenter;
- the **on-premises data gateway**, when only a **managed** connector exists for the data source.

No VPN or ExpressRoute? Use the gateway: it needs **no inbound ports**, only outbound connections from the gateway machine.

Lesson: *Reaching on-premises systems*.
</Step>

<Step id="block-egress" name="Block: controlled egress">
**Need:** all outbound traffic through a central firewall, or one **fixed IP** a partner can allow.

**Add:** a route table to the firewall, with rules built from **service tags** (such as AzureConnectors) and your connections' domain names; a **NAT gateway** for a fixed outbound IP.

**Trade-off:** every missing firewall rule is a broken connector.

Lesson: *Firewalls, fixed IPs and service tags*.
</Step>

<Step id="block-hub" name="Block: shared hub (hub-and-spoke)">
**Need:** several apps share one firewall, one VPN or ExpressRoute link and shared services, run by a central network team.

**Add:** give the app its own network (a **spoke**) and **peer** it to the central **hub**. VNet integration reaches peered networks, including across regions, and calls to a VM or private endpoint there come from the integration subnet's addresses. To share the hub's VPN or ExpressRoute gateway, turn on **gateway transit**: *Allow gateway transit* on the hub's side of the peering and *Use remote gateways* on the spoke's side. Private DNS zones must be linked to the spoke.

Microsoft's hub-and-spoke guidance: keep application workloads, **PaaS included**, in the spokes; the hub holds only shared infrastructure such as the firewall, gateways, Bastion and DNS.

**Trade-off:** routes and firewall rules belong to the hub team, so every new dependency needs a request.

Lessons: *VNet integration* (peered networks) and *Firewalls, fixed IPs and service tags*.
</Step>

<Step id="combos" name="Common combinations" tag="TIP">
Four mixes we see most (ours):

- **SaaS integration**: baseline only.
- **Internal APIs, public triggers**: reach private resources, plus controlled egress when a partner needs a fixed IP.
- **Locked down**: reach private resources + no public inbound + private storage, often with the datacenter and controlled egress blocks.
- **Enterprise**: the locked-down mix inside a **hub-and-spoke** network, with the firewall and the datacenter link in the hub.

Build the outbound block first: the storage and datacenter blocks depend on it.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- The app runs **outside** your network on a Workflow Service Plan; blocks connect it.
- **Reach private resources**: VNet integration (+ private DNS, peering; service endpoints as the lighter option).
- **No public inbound**: private endpoint.
- **Private storage**: four storage private endpoints.
- **Datacenter**: built-in over VPN/ExpressRoute, or the data gateway.
- **Controlled egress**: firewall rules with service tags; NAT gateway for a fixed IP.
- **Shared hub**: a spoke peered to the hub; gateway transit for the hub's VPN; private DNS linked to the spoke.
</Step>
