<Step id="problem" name="Two Service Bus connectors?" tag="PROBLEM">
You add a Service Bus action to a Standard workflow and the designer offers it twice: once under **Built-in** and once under **Shared**.

They're two different connectors, running in two different places. Picking the right one affects speed, cost, networking and how you deploy.
</Step>

<Step id="builtin" name="Built-in: inside the runtime">
**Built-in** connectors run natively on the Logic Apps runtime, **in the same process**. In a Standard workflow, they appear under the **Built-in** filter.

Some are part of the runtime itself, like *Schedule*, *HTTP* and *Request*. Others are informally called **service providers**: built on the Azure Functions extensibility model, they give access to services such as Blob Storage, Service Bus, Event Hubs and SQL Server.
</Step>

<Step id="managed" name="Managed: hosted by Microsoft">
**Managed** connectors are deployed, hosted and managed **in Azure by Microsoft**, on shared resources. Most are a proxy or wrapper around a service's API. They appear under the **Shared** filter.

They usually need you to create a **connection** first and authenticate. There are far more of them: over 1,400, covering services from Office 365 and SharePoint to Salesforce and SAP.
</Step>

<Step id="both" name="Some come in both">
For a smaller set of services, Standard offers **both** versions, for example Azure Blob Storage, Azure Cosmos DB, Azure Event Hubs, Azure Service Bus, FTP, IBM DB2, IBM MQ, SFTP and SQL Server.

Microsoft's guidance: **in most cases** the built-in version provides better performance, capabilities and pricing. Not in every case, though. Check that the operation you need exists in the version you pick.
</Step>

<Step id="cost" name="What each costs">
In Standard, **built-in operations are free**, with no limit; you already pay for the plan.

**Managed** connector operations are billed **per call**, at **Standard** or **Enterprise** connector rates. That applies even when a built-in version of the same connector exists, so choosing built-in is often the cheaper option too.
</Step>

<Step id="connections" name="Where connections live">
A **built-in** connection is part of your logic app's configuration: it lives in `connections.json`, and its secrets are referenced from app settings, for example `@appsetting('serviceBus_connectionString')`.

A **managed** connection is a separate Azure resource, an **API connection**, that you deploy alongside the logic app, for example with an ARM template. `connections.json` references it.
</Step>

<Step id="auth" name="How they authenticate">
Depending on the connector, built-in connections can use a **connection string**, **Microsoft Entra ID** or a **managed identity**. Not all support managed identity: the built-in SFTP connector doesn't, for example. Most service provider connectors currently can't use a user-assigned identity, so they use the **system-assigned** one, which Standard enables by default. SQL Server is an exception.

Microsoft recommends authenticating with a **managed identity** when possible.
</Step>

<Step id="network" name="Reaching private systems">
**Built-in** service provider connectors can reach resources in your **virtual network** and on-premises data sources directly, without the on-premises data gateway.

**Managed** connectors run in multitenant Azure. To reach an on-premises system they need the **on-premises data gateway**. And if your logic app accepts inbound traffic only through a private endpoint, managed webhook triggers and actions won't work: they run in the public cloud and can't call into your private network.
</Step>

<Step id="throttle" name="Throttling" tag="TIP">
Managed connectors have their **own throttling limits**, which vary per connector. For example, the managed Service Bus connector allows up to **6,000 calls per minute**. The service on the other end can throttle you too.

Built-in connectors, hosted on your runtime, are where Microsoft points you for **higher throughput and lower costs at scale**.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- **Built-in** runs in the runtime's process; **managed** is hosted by Microsoft in shared Azure.
- Services such as Service Bus, Blob and SQL Server have both versions; the built-in one is **usually** faster, more capable and cheaper.
- Built-in operations are **free** in Standard; managed calls are billed **per call**.
- Built-in connections live in `connections.json`; managed ones are separate **API connection** resources.
- Built-in reaches your **VNet** and on-premises sources directly; managed needs the **data gateway**.
</Step>
