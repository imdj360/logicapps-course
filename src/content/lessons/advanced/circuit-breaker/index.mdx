<Step id="problem" name="Retrying into a wall" tag="PROBLEM">
Each order calls a partner shipping API. Today it's **down for two hours**.

Every order still calls it, retries **4 times** (the Standard default), waits, and fails. Hundreds of runs pile up, all making the same doomed calls.
</Step>

<Step id="pattern" name="The idea">
**Stop calling a service that keeps failing. Fail fast instead, and check now and then whether it's back.**

Like the circuit breaker in your fuse box: when something is wrong, it cuts the line instead of letting things burn. Microsoft calls this the **Circuit Breaker** pattern.
</Step>

<Step id="states" name="Three states">
- **Closed**: calls go through. The breaker counts recent failures, and **opens** when there are too many in a period.
- **Open**: calls **fail immediately**, without touching the service. After a time-out, it goes half-open.
- **Half-Open**: a **limited number** of calls go through. If they succeed, it closes. If one fails, it opens again.
</Step>

<Step id="sequence" name="How it flows">
1. An order is about to call the partner. It **asks the breaker** first.
2. **Closed**: the call goes through. A failure adds to the count.
3. Too many failures: the breaker **opens**. New orders skip the call and fail fast.
4. After the time-out, a **few test calls** go through. Success closes the breaker.
</Step>

<Step id="build" name="Build it in Logic Apps">
Logic Apps has **no built-in circuit breaker action**, so this is our design:

1. Keep the **state** in a shared place, such as a storage table: Closed or Open, failure count, time opened.
2. Before the partner call, **read the state**. A **Condition** checks it.
3. If **Open**: skip the call and park the order, for example on a queue to retry later.
4. If the call fails, **update the count**. Past your threshold, set the state to Open.
</Step>

<Step id="retry" name="Breaker or retry?">
They do different jobs. **Retry** expects the call to succeed soon. A **breaker** blocks a call that's likely to fail.

Use both: the built-in retry policy handles short glitches, the breaker handles long outages. Don't stack retries inside retries (ours): the attempts and the waiting multiply.
</Step>

<Step id="notneeded" name="When you may not need one" tag="TIP">
Microsoft says a circuit breaker **might not be suitable** in a message-driven or event-driven architecture. Failed messages often go to a dead-letter queue, and built-in isolation and retries are often enough.

So if the partner call already sits behind a queue, start there. Add a breaker (ours) when calls are synchronous and the partner suffers while it's down.
</Step>

<Step id="fallback" name="Fail with something useful" tag="TIP">
An open breaker can return a **default value** that makes sense to the caller, such as "shipping quote on request" (ours) instead of an error.

If recovery time varies a lot, Microsoft suggests a **manual reset** so an administrator can close the breaker.
</Step>

<Step id="when" name="When to use it">
**Use it** when a remote call can fail for a long time, and every extra attempt wastes time or hurts the partner.

**Skip it** when the call is behind a queue with retries and dead-lettering, or when it touches only local resources.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- A **circuit breaker** stops calls to a service that's failing.
- **Closed** counts failures, **Open** fails fast, **Half-Open** tests recovery.
- It works **with** retries: retries for glitches, the breaker for outages.
- In Logic Apps it's **our design**: shared state, a Condition before the call.
- Behind a queue with dead-lettering, you may not need one.
</Step>
