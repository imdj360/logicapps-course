<Step id="problem" name="Retrying into a wall" tag="PROBLEM">
A partner API goes down for two hours. Every order run still calls it, retries four times, waits, and fails. Thousands of runs pile up, each holding resources, each making the same doomed calls.

Microsoft's point: an application **shouldn't keep retrying** an operation that's unlikely to succeed. It should recognise the failure quickly.
</Step>

<Step id="pattern" name="The pattern">
A **circuit breaker** acts as a proxy in front of an operation that might fail. It's a small **state machine** that decides whether a call should even be attempted.
</Step>

<Step id="states" name="Three states">
- **Closed**: calls go through, and the breaker counts recent failures. Too many within a period, and it **opens**.
- **Open**: calls **fail immediately**, without touching the service. After a timeout, it goes half-open.
- **Half-Open**: a **limited number** of calls go through. If they succeed, it closes; if any fails, it opens again.

Half-open stops a recovering service from being flooded the moment it comes back.
</Step>

<Step id="retry" name="Breaker vs retry">
Microsoft separates the two: **retry** expects an operation to succeed eventually; a **circuit breaker** prevents an operation that's likely to fail. They combine well: retry through a breaker, and **stop retrying** when the breaker says the fault isn't transient.

Our caution: stacking retries inside retries multiplies the attempts and the waiting.
</Step>

<Step id="logicapps" name="Around Logic Apps">
Learn has no circuit-breaker guidance for Logic Apps, so this is our design. Keep the breaker's **state** somewhere shared, such as a storage table: closed or open, failure count, time opened.

Before calling the partner, a workflow reads the state. If it's **open**, skip the call and park the work, for example back on a queue for later. After each call, update the count, and open the breaker when failures cross your threshold.
</Step>

<Step id="notneeded" name="When you don't need one" tag="TIP">
Microsoft lists **message-driven and event-driven** architectures as cases where a circuit breaker **might not be suitable**: they often dead-letter failed messages, and built-in isolation and retries are often sufficient.

So if the partner call already sits behind a queue, with sensible retries and dead-lettering, start there. Our rule of thumb: add a breaker when calls are synchronous, expensive, or hurting the partner while they're down.
</Step>

<Step id="fallback" name="Fail with something useful" tag="TIP">
Microsoft notes the open state can return a **default value** that's meaningful to the caller, for example (ours) "price on request" instead of an error. If recovery time is very variable, it suggests a **manual reset** option for administrators.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- A **circuit breaker** stops calls to a service that's failing.
- **Closed** counts failures, **Open** fails fast, **Half-Open** tests recovery.
- Combine it with **retries**; don't retry through an open breaker.
- In Logic Apps it's your design: shared state, checked before each call.
- Behind a queue with dead-lettering, you may not need one.
</Step>
