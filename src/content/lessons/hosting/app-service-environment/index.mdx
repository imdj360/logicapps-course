<Step id="problem" name="Nothing shared" tag="PROBLEM">
The security team has a new rule for the invoicing workflows: they must run on **dedicated** compute, with **no public endpoints**, inside the company network.

Private endpoints on a Workflow Service Plan get you part of the way. For full isolation, Standard can run in an **App Service Environment v3**.
</Step>

<Step id="what" name="A dedicated environment">
An App Service Environment is a **fully isolated and dedicated** environment for running App Service apps, and Standard logic apps are one of the supported workloads.

For Standard you choose an **App Service plan** in the **Isolated v2** tier, and **Windows plans only**. ASE v1 and v2 are no longer supported for Standard logic apps.
</Step>

<Step id="network" name="Internal or external">
An ASE has one of two kinds of inbound address:

- **Internal**: the inbound address is inside the ASE's subnet, so apps are reached through your virtual network, and any networks connected to it, not from the public internet.
- **External**: the inbound address is public, and apps are listed in public DNS.

With an internal ASE, your workflows can reach resources in the connected virtual network, and their endpoints are internal only. That's the security team's answer.
</Step>

<Step id="history" name="Run history from outside" tag="PROBLEM">
One surprise: if you open run history from **outside** an internal ASE, for example in the Azure portal on a laptop that isn't on the network, the run history **can't show action inputs and outputs**.

Plan for it: your team needs network access to the ASE to troubleshoot runs properly.
</Step>

<Step id="scaling" name="You own the scaling">
There's **no dynamic scaling** in an ASE. You set **scaling rules** on the App Service plan yourself, or scale it manually.

A commonly used rule, per Microsoft, is to scale on the **CPU metric** and keep virtual CPU between **50% and 70%**. One Isolated v2 plan supports up to **100 instances**, and one ASE up to **200 instances** across all its plans.
</Step>

<Step id="billing" name="Paying for the plan">
You pay for the **App Service plan's instances**, no matter how many logic apps you run on it.

The environment itself has a floor: while no instances are deployed, an empty ASE is charged as if it runs **one Windows I1v2 instance**. Either way you pay for dedicated capacity whether or not it's busy, so ASE makes most sense when you run many apps in it or need its isolation anyway.
</Step>

<Step id="differences" name="Other differences" tag="TIP">
A few details that differ from a Workflow Service Plan:

- **Storage account key access**: on an ASE you can disable it once you've set up managed identity authentication. On a Workflow Service Plan you currently can't.
- **Runtime Scale Monitoring** isn't supported on an ASE.
- **Zone redundancy** is available in regions that support it. For the logic app you turn it on when you create the resource, and there's no extra charge for it on the ASE.
</Step>

<Step id="when" name="When it's worth it" tag="TIP">
Choose ASE v3 when isolation is a **requirement**, not a preference: dedicated compute, internal-only endpoints, and full control over network traffic.

If private endpoints and VNet integration on a Workflow Service Plan meet your security rules, they're simpler to run, and you keep dynamic scaling.
</Step>

<Step id="recap" name="Recap" tag="RECAP">
- ASE v3 is a **fully isolated, dedicated** environment; Standard uses an **Isolated v2** App Service plan, **Windows only**.
- **Internal** ASE: inbound address inside your subnet. **External**: public address and public DNS.
- From outside an internal ASE, run history **can't show inputs and outputs**.
- **No dynamic scaling**: set rules on the plan, for example 50–70% CPU. Up to 100 instances per plan, 200 per ASE.
- You pay for the **plan's instances**; an empty ASE still costs one I1v2 instance.
</Step>
